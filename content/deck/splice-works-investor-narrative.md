## Canonical one-paragraph narrative

> Splice Works deploys governed AI infrastructure into mid-market and enterprise operations. We install Splice Station, specialized harnesses, and client-specific brains — starting from one high-value problem, live in two weeks, shipped in thirty days. Every engagement ships a working system. What we learn hardens the platform for the next one.

**Thesis line** (used as the slide 10 headline):

> Splice Works' edge is not that it can build an agent. It is that it can deploy governed, portable, maintainable agentic systems into a real business quickly — then compound what it learns into the next deployment.

## Slide 2 — Problem → Approach

**Eyebrow:** The category has a delivery problem

**Headline:** Most companies have the tools. Almost none have the results.

**Body:** A tool without governance, ownership, and a path to production is a demo. The difference between a stalled pilot and a running system is one thing: someone stayed until it shipped.

**Diagram — two paths:**

| Buy tools | Deploy infrastructure |
|---|---|
| Copilot / chatbot purchased | Station installed, one scoped problem |
| Pilot with one team | Harness proven in sandbox & shadow mode |
| **Stalls** — no owner, no audit trail, vendor exits | **Shipped**, gated, and maintained |

---

## Slide 3 — What we deploy

**Eyebrow:** The stack

**Headline:** Three layers. One environment.

**Body:** Everything is built for Station first, then deployed to the client. The client gets a product. We get a reusable asset.

**Diagram — stack (top to bottom):**
- **Brains** — the client's data, process, and constraints, encoded so agents act correctly inside their business — updated continuously, not built once.
- **Harnesses** — scoped agent systems for one workflow: chatbot hardening, legacy rewrite, lead generation, workflow modernization.
- **Splice Station** — the control plane. Orchestrates agents, enforces approval gates, tracks every decision and artifact.

---

## Slide 4 — Delivery cadence & proof

**Eyebrow:** How we ship

**Headline:** Station in two weeks. Nothing writes until it's proven.

**Body:** We start with one concrete problem, not a strategy deck. Every harness is proven against real client data before it gets write access. Gates stay on after go-live — permanently.

**Diagram — pipeline:**
Week 1–2 Station live → Sandbox (built, tested on real data) → Shadow mode (runs, proposes, doesn't act) → Month 1 Production (write access granted) → Ongoing (gates stay on, maintenance agents run)

**Diagram — time compression:** 24 months of harness R&D (long bar) compressing into a 30-day client deliverable (short bar).

**Line:** You're buying a 30-day deliverable, backed by 24 months of harness engineering — not funding a six-month build.

## Slide 5 — Why it's different

**Eyebrow:** The differentiation

**Headline:** Governed, portable, and still running after we leave.

**Diagram — 5 icons:**
- **Governed** — every action logged, gated, attributable.
- **Portable** — agents move to the next harness, not rebuilt each time.
- **Persistent** — maintained and improved after deployment.
- **Omni-channel** — Slack, Teams, Claude, WhatsApp, or a custom UI.
- **Deployed your way** — cloud SaaS by default; bring-your-own-cloud or on-prem available.

## Slide 7 — Unit economics

**Eyebrow:** How the model runs today

**Headline:** FDE-heavy by design — for now.

**Body:** Forward-deployed engineers embedded with clients — not just strong engineers, but people who turn an executive's problem into a harness.

**Diagram — stats + bar chart:**
- 3 FDEs per ~$75K engagement revenue
- ~$36K net after delivery cost
- Current $75K/mo → target $200K/mo before adding ~3 heads

**Footnote:** Internal planning estimates, not audited. Shown to illustrate the delivery ratio, not a forecast.

---

## Slide 8 — The tension, named directly

**Eyebrow:** What could go wrong, and why it won't

**Headline:** Growth without productization is just a bigger consultancy.

**Body:** This only works if what we learn on engagement N makes engagement N+1 faster and less headcount-dependent.

**Diagram — trap vs. flywheel:**

| The trap | The flywheel |
|---|---|
| More clients signed | More clients signed |
| More embedded FDEs required | More reusable harnesses & portable agents |
| Margin compresses per client | Less headcount per new deployment |
| Pigeonholed as a staffing business | Platform business, not a services business |
| | ↺ hardens next engagement (shown as a closed loop, not a list) |

**Diagram — the moat** (5 numbered chips, under the trap/flywheel diagram):
1. Cross-harness optimization
2. Governance substrate (Station)
3. Portable agents
4. Ongoing brains & maintenance
5. Compounding vertical intelligence

**Line (internal framing, not for the chips):** Individual harnesses may commoditize. The moat is implementation, enterprise integration, and making the systems work together.
