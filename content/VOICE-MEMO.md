# Voice memo — Do's, Don'ts, and the review protocol

This memo is the working reference for anyone or any agent drafting Splice Works copy. Check a draft against this *before* writing, and run the humanizer pass *after* every draft or revision. `VOICE.md` holds the five core rules; this memo expands them into checkable Do's and Don'ts.

## The five rules

1. Say it simple.
2. Say it short.
3. No sentences about sentences. Get to the point in as few words as possible.
4. Rich, dense, brief.
5. Tone is confident and inviting.

---

## Do

- Lead with the point. Support it after.
- One idea per sentence.
- Concrete verbs and nouns. "Install," "ship," "gate" — not "leverage," "enable," "facilitate."
- A number beats an adjective. "30 days," not "quickly." "Four stages," not "several stages."
- Active voice, active subject. "We install Station," not "Station is installed."
- Cut the sentence in half. Then cut it again.
- End on the strongest word.
- State the claim plainly. "Splice Works deploys X," not "Splice Works aims to help deploy X."
- Address the reader directly — "you," "we." Invite, don't lecture.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. A short line after a longer one lands harder.
- Read it out loud before calling it done. If it sounds like a person, keep it.

## Don't

**AI-slop tells — cut on sight:**
- Throat-clearing openers: "In today's fast-evolving landscape...", "It's worth noting that...", "As we all know..."
- Sentences about the sentence: "Simply put," "In other words," "This section covers," "To put it another way"
- Rule-of-three padding used to *sound* thorough rather than *be* thorough — one sharp example beats three soft ones
- Hedge-stacking: "might potentially," "could possibly help," "in many cases may"
- Paired-adjective inflation: "robust and scalable," "seamless and intuitive," "innovative and cutting-edge"
- The "not just X, but Y" construction, more than once per document
- Corporate abstractions: "synergize," "leverage," "unlock," "supercharge," "seamless," "robust," "cutting-edge," "game-changing," "revolutionize," "delve," "elevate"
- Filler transitions: "That said," "At the end of the day," "Moving forward," "It goes without saying"
- Rhetorical questions used as transitions: "So how do we solve this?"
- Restating one claim three different ways in three consecutive sentences
- Exclamation marks. Emoji.
- Title Case Headings — sentence case only.
- "I" — always "we" for Splice Works, "you" for the reader.
- A claim with no number and no mechanism behind it.

---

## The review protocol

**Before drafting:** re-read this memo. Pull the facts from the canonical source doc (`SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md` or the approved narrative doc) — don't invent claims, numbers, or partner names that aren't already sourced.

**After every draft or revision, in order:**

1. **Voice check** — walk the Do/Don't lists above line by line. Flag anything on the Don't list. Fix or cut.
2. **Blade Humanizer pass** — smooth rhythm, vary sentence length, remove any line that sounds like it was generated rather than said. A human should be able to read the whole thing aloud in one breath per sentence.
3. **No AI Slop pass** — final sweep for the specific tells above: hedge-stacking, paired-adjective inflation, throat-clearing, meta-commentary, rule-of-three padding.
4. **Read-aloud test** — if a sentence describes what the writing is doing, or you stumble reading it aloud, cut it or rewrite it shorter.

A draft isn't done until it has passed all three (voice check, humanizer, no-slop) — not just the first one.
