# You have AI tools, or a mandate to get them. The work hasn't changed yet.

This is step 1, from AI-absent to AI-enabled. We set up a governed AI workspace early and build your company's shared brain, starting with what your experts know. Along the way, your people start using agents in their real work. The step ends when your first critical workflow is started and run by agents. Step 1 proves the pattern on one workflow; step 2 makes it how your company runs.

## You're here if you've bought the licenses and nothing changed

- "We rolled out Copilot and ChatGPT. Nothing about how the work gets done changed."
- "We ran pilots. None reached production."
- "The CEO said we're becoming an AI company. Nobody has defined what that means, or what we're allowed to automate."
- "What our best people know lives in one or two heads, and getting it wrong has real consequences."

That's AI-absent: you may have the tools, but the work runs the way it always has.

## What we build with you

AI-enabled means everything your company knows feeds one shared brain your people use every day, and your first critical workflow is started and run by agents. We're forward-deployed, so we build it with your people, inside the systems you already run.

- **A governed AI workspace, early.** Your people and agents work there, with spend caps, a kill switch and every action logged.
- A prompt library and a skills library, checked against your policies. Each addition gets announced, so good work spreads.
- **Your company's brain**, fed by your data and by what your experts know, so that knowledge reaches everyone who needs it. See [how we're doing this for a manufacturer](/industries/manufacturing).
- Agents across your teams, custom or off-the-shelf, whichever fits. Bring your own agents or use ours.
- Short training in plain language: what a prompt is, what a skill is, and how to write both. People get fluent by using it on their own work.

## Step 1 ends when your first critical workflow runs on agents

A critical workflow recurs and matters to the business, and someone still starts it by hand. A one-off prompt or a demo doesn't count. We choose it with you. Some examples:

| Function | Agents start and run | A person decides at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales | A new inbound lead comes in: agents research and enrich it, draft a reply and book the meeting | Outreach to strategic accounts |
| Support | A ticket comes in: agents triage it, resolve it from the brain if it's tier-1, or draft the reply | Refunds or credits above a set amount |
| Finance | An invoice arrives: agents match it to its purchase order and flag any exception | Payments and write-offs |
| People ops | A new hire is added to the HR system: agents set up accounts, training and a schedule | Access to sensitive systems |
| Reporting | Every Monday, agents pull the numbers, draft the report and post it | Anything going outside the company |

Before anything runs, we agree who is accountable and what each agent may approve.
