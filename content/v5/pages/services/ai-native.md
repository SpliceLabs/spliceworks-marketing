# Your first workflow runs on agents. Now make it how your company runs.

This is step 2, from AI-enabled to AI-native. We rebuild your critical workflows so your company's brain drives the work. Agents become coworkers: they do much of the work and escalate to your people based on your policies, and there's an accountable person behind every decision that matters.

## You're here if most work still waits to be asked

Your company's knowledge feeds a shared brain your people use every day, and at least one critical workflow already runs on agents. Everywhere else, work still starts with someone opening a chat and typing a prompt. There, the brain answers questions and never starts anything.

The working session confirms where you are. If this is you, you skip step 1.

## What changes when the brain drives the work

| | AI-enabled | AI-native |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who starts the work | A person, with a prompt | Agents, on triggers, schedules and loops |
| The brain's role | Something people consult | The engine that drives the work |
| Your people | Do the work, with AI helping | Make the calls that need a person, and set what agents may approve |
| How it's counted | Daily AI use | Critical workflows run by agents, one by one |

## What we do in step 2

- **Rebuild your workflows around the brain.** We redesign the work for people and agents together, rather than bolting automation onto the old process.
- Agents pick up work from triggers, schedules and loops, with no prompt needed. They work as coworkers, doing much of the work and escalating to your people based on your policies.
- **Approval points you set.** Your people decide where it matters, and set what agents may approve everywhere else.
- Spend caps, a kill switch and every action logged, on every workflow.

Step 1 got one workflow running on its own. Step 2 does the same for the workflows your business depends on.

## People stay accountable at the gates that matter

Some approvals belong with a person. Others can go to an agent that an accountable person has permissioned. Before anything runs, we agree which is which and who is accountable for each decision. We also say plainly what the AI controls and what it only observes.

## Questions before step 2

### Who's accountable when the AI gets it wrong?

A person on your side. There's an accountable person behind every decision that matters, and agents can handle some approvals that person has permissioned. Before anything runs, we agree who is accountable and what each agent may approve.

### Won't this just raise our AI bill?

It shouldn't. Any vendor can raise your usage. We design for value that grows faster than your AI bill.

### Can we use our own agents?

Yes. No vendor lock-in: bring your own agents or use ours, and swap them whenever you want.
