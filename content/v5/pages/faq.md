# Questions, answered plainly

What buyers ask us before they start, and what we tell them. If yours isn't here, ask us in a [working session](/book).

## Why us, and why not another route

### Why not do this in-house?

You can, and we'll leave your team able to. We make the path shorter because we've done this before and we bring the governance infrastructure. The same team stays with you from the first session to production.

### Why not a company-brain platform?

A platform asks you to trust the machine. We start from your own experts and cite where every answer comes from, with an accountable person behind every decision that matters.

### Why not a consultancy?

A consultancy bills weeks of analysis and leaves you a deck. You get something usable in the first working session, and that session is credited in full if you go on to work with us.

### Are we too small for this?

No. We work with solo founders as well as large teams.

### Which service is right for us?

It depends on where you are today. If you have the tools but the work hasn't changed, even if people use them daily, start with [AI-absent → AI-enabled](/services/ai-enabled). If your first critical workflow already runs on agents but most work still waits to be asked, start with [AI-enabled → AI-native](/services/ai-native). If you need AI leadership on your team first, look at [fractional leadership](/services/fractional). If you're not sure, the working session will tell you which fits.

### Why a fractional seat instead of a full-time hire?

A full-time AI executive is hard to find and slow to hire, and most companies don't yet know what the permanent role should be. A fractional seat puts someone accountable for AI on your leadership team now, delivers the first outcomes, and can write the mandate for the permanent role if you want one.

## Accountability and safety

### Who's accountable when the AI gets it wrong?

A person on your side. There's an accountable person behind every decision that matters, and agents can handle some approvals that person has permissioned. Before anything runs, we agree who is accountable and what each agent may approve.

### Is our data safe?

Your data stays in the systems you already use. We work inside them, with only the access you give us, under NDA.

### What if it doesn't work?

We don't offer a guarantee. You see a working prototype on one of your own workflows in the first session, before you commit to more, and we tell you early if a workflow isn't working.

## The cost of waiting on AI

### What is the cost of delay for AI?

It's the value you give up each month an AI opportunity isn't running. Add up what each opportunity would be worth per month, then multiply by the months you wait. That money is gone before you spend anything on implementation. [Work out yours](/tools/ai-cost-of-delay).

### How do you calculate the cost of delaying AI adoption?

Multiply the number of AI opportunities by the monthly value of each one. That's your monthly cost of delay. Multiply it by the months until you'd go live to get the cumulative cost. We also show half of that as a conservative case, because value usually ramps up rather than arriving on day one. [Run the numbers](/tools/ai-cost-of-delay).

### How do I estimate the monthly value of an AI opportunity?

Start with one workflow. Count the hours your people spend on it each month and multiply by what those hours cost you. Add any revenue it would bring in sooner or errors it would stop. If you can't defend a number, use the low end.

### Isn't it cheaper to wait until AI matures?

Waiting has a cost too, it's just harder to see. Every month you wait, the value of that work goes unclaimed and your team learns nothing. The tools will keep improving either way. Start on one real workflow now and build it so you can swap tools later without starting over.

### How do I make the case for AI to my CEO or board?

Show them what waiting costs, in dollars, on work they recognize. Pick one to three workflows, put a monthly value on each and state the cost of a 6-month delay. Then name who is accountable for each one. [The calculator](/tools/ai-cost-of-delay) writes a line you can paste into the deck.

## Working out the ROI of AI

### How do you calculate the ROI of AI?

Add up what the rollout costs in year one: tools, training, infrastructure and the dip in output while people learn. Then work out the capacity it frees: people × cost per person × the share of time AI saves. Net benefit is that capacity minus the cost, and ROI is net benefit divided by the cost. [Run the numbers](/tools/ai-roi-calculator).

### Is time saved by AI real money?

Only if you use it. Freed hours become money when they go into work you'd otherwise hire for, ship later or not do at all. If nobody decides where the time goes, it disappears into the week. Name the work it goes to before you count it.

### How much time does AI really save?

It depends on the work, so measure one workflow before you commit. Our calculator starts at 12.5% of each person's time, the default in DORA's AI ROI calculator. Treat that as a starting point, not a promise, and use your own number once you have it.

### Why does AI make teams slower before they get faster?

People need time to learn new tools and change how they work, so output dips first. That dip is a real cost, and it belongs in the business case. Keep it short by starting with one workflow and the people who own it.

### How do you keep the productivity dip small?

Don't put everyone through it at once. Start with one workflow and the few people who own it, build a working prototype of it in one focused day, and measure what it really saves. Then roll out to the next team, faster each time. The rest of the team gets its gains later, but you find out what AI saves before you commit the full budget.

### How long does AI take to pay back?

Divide the first-year cost by the monthly value of the time it frees, then add the months the dip lasts, since nothing is saved until it ends. With our calculator's defaults, a 50-person team pays back in about 13 months. Your own numbers will move that a lot, which is why we test them on a real workflow.

## Getting ready for AI

### How do I know if my company is ready for AI?

Look at whether the work has changed, not whether people have tools. Pick one recurring workflow and ask how far AI goes in it: does it help people, do steps, or start and run the workflow with people deciding at set points? Then check the foundations: an owner, AI that knows your business, a way to check its work, and a way to measure what changes. [Take the check-up](/tools/ai-check-up).

### What's the difference between AI-enabled and AI-native?

AI-enabled means at least one critical workflow is started and run by agents, with your people deciding at the points that matter. AI-native means that's how the company runs: agents run the workflows that run your business.

### Why ask about foundations as well as AI use?

Because a claim needs something to hold it up. If agents run a workflow but nobody owns AI and nothing is measured, the workflow is hard to trust and hard to improve. The check-up counts the rung your foundations support, not the one you claim.

### What should we fix first?

The foundation that holds you back most, which is rarely another tool. If nobody owns AI, start there. If AI's work isn't checked or measured, people won't trust it to run a workflow. Then pick one workflow and change how it runs.
