## 2. Why Pulse & Patterns maps best, and where we fall short

Their page walks a buyer through: the problem, the role, the mandate, the time, the deliverables, the exit, when to hire permanently, FAQs. Ours opens with the offer and the outcomes, which is stronger as a plan but weaker as an explanation. A reader who has never heard of a fractional CAIO has nothing to hold on to.

Missing from our page today:
1. **The problem.** Who owns AI decisions now, and what happens when no one does.
2. **The role, in plain terms.** What the seat does and does not do (signs nothing? holds budget? reports to whom?). Our mandate step says "decision rights" but never shows an example.
3. **A decision-rights table.** Pulse maps six areas to "what the seat contributes" versus "what your executives keep". This is the most reusable thing on their page.
4. **Time commitment.** We say nothing. Every comparable page at least says "agreed in the mandate" and describes the shape (heavier first, tapering).
5. **How it ends.** We have "hand over to a permanent hire" in one line. Pulse and HatchWorks spell out exit as a feature.
6. **When to hire full-time instead.** Pulse, Product Craft and HatchWorks all say this. It builds trust and costs nothing.
7. **Seat versus consultant versus CIO/CTO.** Three FAQs every comparable page answers.
8. **Fit for people who are not leaders of technology.** We have "for founders" and "for technology leaders" links but no line on the CEO/board reader the page is written for.

Where we are ahead, so keep and sharpen: written outcomes with baselines, a quarterly renew/expand/hand-over review, the spend policy plus accountability map (the "AI bill" angle), the credited working session, and the "Ask your AI about this page" prompt. Nobody else has these.

## 4. Offering and tiers

Market shapes we saw (all vendor-published, unverified): Vizion $2.5k / $5k / $10k a month at 4 / 10 / 20 hours; Fractionus $10k to $22k (US); Umbrex $15k to $30k for one to three days a week; HatchWorks $5k to $30k (its own two ranges disagree). Product Craft has no price but a clear ladder: 2 to 3 days a week (seat), 2 to 4 hours a week (advisor), monthly mentorship.

What this suggests for us. Today we have two seats plus advisory and no price. A ladder buyers can read:

| Shape | What it is | Time (proposal, to confirm) | Entry point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Working session | A paid day, credited if you continue | One day | Booking page |
| Advisory | Monthly sessions and answers between them; no seat | Hours per month | Session, or direct |
| Seat (CAIO or CPTO/CTO) | Accountable for written quarterly outcomes | Days per week, agreed in the mandate | After the session |
| Seat plus engagement | Seat alongside our AI-absent to AI-native work | Per engagement | From the seat |

Our stance: **outcomes, not hours, define the seat; time bands describe capacity.** That fits the written success plan and is the clearest difference from Vizion (hours) and Product Craft (days).

Decisions we need from you:
1. Publish a price or "from" figure, or keep "talk to us"? Every comparable page except Vizion hides it, so publishing is a differentiator, but only if the number is firm. This is open item #3 (session price).
2. Do we state the time bands (for example "one to two days a week")? Needs capacity confirmation from the team.
3. Is the 90-day first quarter a fixed-scope start with a renew decision at day 90? HatchWorks, Umbrex and Product Craft all do this; it lowers the commitment barrier.
4. Advisory shape and response time (#23).

## 5. FAQs to add

Current: three (why fractional, cost, too small). Proposed additions, grouped. Answer notes are direction only; all answers need founder confirmation, as with the rest of the FAQ.

**The role**
- What is a fractional Chief AI Officer? (plain definition, two sentences)
- Who would we actually work with?
- Does the seat replace our CIO or CTO? (no; sits beside)
- What is the difference between the seat and an AI consultant? (the seat answers for outcomes agreed in writing; a consultant delivers recommendations)
- Is the seat an officer of our company? (decision rights are set in the mandate; say what the seat can and cannot sign)
- What is the difference between a CAIO, a CTO and a chief data officer?

**How it works**
- How much time do we get?
- Does the seat include implementation, or only direction? (be exact about what we build)
- What does the first 90 days look like?
- How do you measure whether the seat is working? (link to the success plan)
- How does it end? (handover, extend, or step down to advisory)
- Can we start with advisory and move up?

**Fit**
- When should we hire a full-time AI executive instead?
- Our board or investors are asking about AI. Can the seat represent us?
- Do you work with our existing vendors, or do you recommend your own? (independence; Kalosys and Pulse both answer this because both sell implementation)

**Money**
- What does it cost? (replace "talk to us" once decision 1 is made)
- Do we have to keep paying you? (ties to #19 in the open items)
