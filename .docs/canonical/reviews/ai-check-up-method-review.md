# AI check-up: measurement and plain-language review

## Framing

The check-up is a five-minute lead tool that one leader answers on behalf of the whole company. It can't be a validated instrument, and the methodologist is right that it produces "precise-looking" results from compressed self-report. The aim here is narrower:

- the result should be **honest and hard to game**;
- it should **not flatten different companies into the same answer**;
- every line should **read the same way to everyone**.

A validated assessment belongs in the gated full breakdown.

## A. Quick wins: wording, with no scoring change

| # | Change | From |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Area stems anchor to regular use over the last month: "In the last month, how far did AI go in X? Pick the furthest it goes in regular work, not one-off trials." | Both |
| A2 | Level 4 detail: "AI starts the work and sees it through. People make the key decisions." Drop "set points". | Plain language |
| A3 | Use one term per idea: AI (agent only where it's defined), work vs workflow, people. Say "level", not "rung", everywhere. | Plain language |
| A4 | Add a "Words we use" block (workflow, AI agent, foundations, level) above Q1. Add a bridge line before Q5. | Plain language |
| A5 | Q5 bands stop overlapping: under 20%, 20 to 39%, 40 to 59%, 60 to 79%, 80% or more. | Plain language |
| A6 | Q6: "Who is in charge of AI?" | Plain language |
| A7 | Q8 hint: "If more than one fits, pick the one that covers most of your AI use." | Both |
| A8 | Q11: make the top option cumulative ("A written policy, plus ..."). | Both |
| A9 | Gaps line: "In N of your 4 areas, you said AI goes further than your foundations can support yet, so we've scored those areas lower." | Plain language |
| A10 | Shorten the page's foundations sentence and its threshold paragraph to bring the reading age down. | Plain language |

## B. Cheap accuracy wins: small logic, little burden

| # | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Contradiction prompt before the result.** If any area is at level 4 or above while workflowChange is 2 or below, or any area is at 5 while measurement is below 5, ask the person to confirm or change their answer. | Today the tool quietly caps answers it should be questioning. |
| B2 | **Show the level for each area** on the result, for example "Core work: does steps (you said runs the workflow)". | Two very different profiles currently show the same 2.4 and the same copy. |
| B3 | **Show the score as a whole level, or drop it.** Keep the stage. | The decimal suggests interval precision that isn't there. Needs the founder's call. |

## C. Founder calls: scoring design

| # | Change | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | When an area is at level 4 or 5, ask a follow-up: "Has it run in normal use in the last month?" No or don't know caps that area at 3. | Adds up to 4 short questions, but stops the most common over-claim. |
| C2 | Replace the eight-foundation average with gates: level 4 needs an owner, approved access, defined checking and a changed workflow; level 5 also needs measurement. | Stops a strong foundation hiding a missing one. It's a redesign and it sets the operating bar. |
| C3 | Add "Don't know" to the foundations and mark the result provisional. | More honest, but needs copy and handling for a provisional result. |
| C4 | Split the bundled foundations (ownership, context, access, rules) into single-idea questions. | More questions. Better done in the gated full breakdown. |

## Declined

- **Randomising option order and using neutral labels.** This hurts usability for little gain on a short ladder whose order is obvious.
- **One named workflow per area, typed in.** Too much friction for a no-signup tool. A1 gets most of the benefit.
- **Role and confidence questions.** Better suited to the gated breakdown.
- **Calling the session "Book a working day" everywhere.** "Working session" is the site-wide name.
