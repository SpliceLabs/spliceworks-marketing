The ladder is sound. A non-technical buyer can finish it. The failures are at the choices most companies actually face, and in a few result lines that talk past the answer they just gave.

Persona used for the picks: a 120-person firm, CEO not technical. A handful of people use ChatGPT to draft. Nothing is connected. Nobody owns it. No policy. The work runs as before.

## The 12 questions

### 1. Core work
**Pick:** rung 2, with a real hesitation at rung 1. Drafting a proposal in ChatGPT is both "chat on their own" and "drafts, summarises and researches".
**Issues:** "How far" asks for the best anecdote, including a pilot. Rungs 1 and 2 overlap, so the modal company is sorted by mood. "Agents" in rungs 4 and 5 is jargon, but those details are otherwise clear. Bought AI inside software they already pay for (Copilot, the CRM, the accounts package) has no obvious home. The climb from 3 to 5 matches the scores.
**Verdict:** TWEAK. Same replacement options for questions 2 to 4.

**In your core work, the thing you make or deliver, which of these is true on a normal week?**
- **[1] It isn't part of the work** / Not used, or people only chat with it on the side
- **[2] It helps people** / People use it in the job to draft, summarise and research. They still do the work.
- **[3] It does steps** / It does a set part of the job, the same way each time. A person starts it and finishes it.
- **[4] It runs the workflow** / It starts and runs the work by itself. People decide only at set points.
- **[5] It improves the workflow** / It runs the work end to end and changes it from the results. People set the goals.

Count AI inside a product you already use only if people actually use it in the work.

### 2. Sales, marketing and support
**Pick:** rung 2, little hesitation. This is where ad hoc use already is.
**Issues:** same 1/2 blur. A CEO will answer for marketing and ignore support. That matches a "how far" reading, so leave it.
**Verdict:** TWEAK. Same options. Stem: **In sales, marketing and support, which of these is true on a normal week?**

### 3. Finance, people and admin
**Pick:** rung 1. Hesitated because the accounts package now "has AI". If nobody uses it, rung 1 is honest.
**Issues:** same options. Vendor AI bites hardest here.
**Verdict:** TWEAK. Stem: **In finance, people and admin, which of these is true on a normal week?**

### 4. Reporting, planning and decisions
**Pick:** rung 2. Hesitated at rung 1. Pasting the numbers into a chat to draft a commentary is help, not a side chat, and it is not a step that runs every month.
**Issues:** same options. "Decisions" tempts a CEO to score their own chatting as company practice. "On a normal week" is the constraint.
**Verdict:** TWEAK. Stem: **In reporting, planning and decisions, which of these is true on a normal week?**

### 5. How many people
**Pick:** Under 20%. Hesitated. A CEO does not know the percentage and will guess high. The bands are wide enough to survive that.
**Issues:** "Your people" in a 20 to 500 person firm that is not a software firm includes warehouse, shop-floor, care and field staff. An office that uses AI every week can still score 1. That pulls the foundation average down and caps every area. The result then feels unfair, and unfair results do not get booked. The bands themselves climb properly.
**Verdict:** TWEAK. Keep the bands.

**Of the people whose work is mostly documents, email or a computer, how many use AI in that work every week, beyond the odd chat?**

### 6. Who owns AI
**Pick:** Informal champions. Hesitated toward "someone, part-time" if IT was told to "look after it".
**Issues:** The jump from 2 to 4 is where most firms sit: a named person, no time. They will round up, and the foundation score moves with them. "Owns" is the right word. The top option is specific enough to resist flattery.
**Verdict:** TWEAK. Keep the scores.

- **[1]** Nobody yet
- **[2]** Keen people, but it is nobody's job
- **[4]** One person has it as part of their job
- **[5]** A leader or team, with time and a budget

### 7. What AI knows
**Pick:** Whatever someone pastes in. Little hesitation.
**Issues:** The middle option, "documents we've connected", sounds like an IT project. A shared brief, a project, or a folder of notes is the real step before that, and it is the thing you later sell. Those buyers are scored as paste-in. "Systems" here also collides with question 8. The top rung is a fair high bar.
**Verdict:** TWEAK.

**When AI helps with your work, what does it know about your business?**
- **[1]** Only what someone pastes in each time
- **[3]** A shared set of notes or documents we keep for it
- **[5]** Our documents, our data and our experts' know-how, kept up to date

### 8. How AI reaches systems
**Pick:** Copy and paste. Then a stall. A CEO cannot tell "approved integrations" from "scoped access" and does not know what a broad key is. This is the question that loses them.
**Issues:** Two answers score 1, but the list reads as a ladder, so a shared login looks like progress. It is also the dangerous one. "Scoped access" and "audit trail" are security jargon. People will tick the bottom one to look competent.
**Verdict:** REWRITE. If the layout still looks like a scale, the second option has to say it is not an upgrade.

**Which of these is true of your files and systems?**
- **[1]** People copy and paste into AI
- **[1]** AI uses a shared login, or a login that can see far too much. This is not further along than copy and paste.
- **[4]** AI is connected only to tools we've approved
- **[5]** AI can see only what each task needs, and we can check what it did

### 9. How the work is checked
**Pick:** wanted "each person decides". The current middle option nudged me to "a person reviews everything", because that sounds like the responsible answer. Most firms will do the same.
**Issues:** "Each person decides" and "a person reviews everything" overlap when the user checks their own output. That flatters the foundation score by two points. "Before it counts" is vague. The top option is clear and rare enough.
**Verdict:** TWEAK.

**How is AI's work checked before you rely on it?**
- **[1]** The person who used it decides
- **[3]** Someone else reviews it before it is used
- **[5]** The system checks it, and a person approves only at set points

### 10. Measurement
**Pick:** Not measured. No hesitation.
**Issues:** "Baseline" is the one jargon word. Otherwise this is the cleanest question in the set. The top is specific enough that people will not fake it.
**Verdict:** TWEAK the last option only.

- **[1]** Not measured
- **[3]** Rough estimates
- **[5]** Yes, for each workflow, against how it ran before

### 11. Rules
**Pick:** None yet. Mild hesitation if HR once sent an email. A verbal "use the paid account" is still none yet.
**Issues:** None that matter. The top bundle is concrete, and "a way to switch an agent off" keeps firms with no agents from ticking it. Leave "agent" here.
**Verdict:** KEEP.

### 12. Has the work changed
**Pick:** Not yet. No hesitation. This is the question that states your point of view in the buyer's language.
**Issues:** "Didn't reach production" means the factory to a manufacturer and means nothing to a law firm. The jump from a trial (2) to one live workflow (4) is right. Do not add a middle.
**Verdict:** TWEAK option 2 only.

- **[2]** We ran trials, and they didn't become how we work

## Result copy

Keep the stage names. "AI-absent" is blunt, and the meaning line is what softens it. Keep the rung 1 and rung 2 meaning lines, the AI-enabled meaning line, the "one workflow away" line, and both prototype next-step lines. Those land.

**Close to AI-enabled.** The current line mentions a mandate they never told you about. They said AI does steps.

> AI does steps of the work in at least one area. You don't yet have a workflow that starts and runs by itself, with your people deciding at set points.

**AI-native meaning.** "Foundations" arrives before you've explained it.

> The work that runs your business is started and run by agents, and the basics are in place to trust that.

**Gaps.** "Claim" accuses them of exaggerating at the moment you want the booking.

> Your answers match what those basics can support.
> In N of the 4 areas, the answers are ahead of what those basics can support.

**AI-absent next step.** Right offer, wrong words. "Prompting" and "markdown" sound like a tools class for someone junior. The CEO will not book that.

> In one focused day we'll get your people briefing AI properly, writing down the context it needs so it gets reused, and organising the work so it builds up over time.

Nothing here insults a low score beyond the heading, which you should keep. Nothing oversells except a one-day prototype, and that is the offer, so leave it.

## Page copy

The hero, the stage definitions, the worked example, the "leaves out" section and the FAQ are doing the job. Leave them.

1. **Insider sentence in "How the check-up works".** "Agentic maturity model" teaches nothing and asks them to trust a private framework. Replace with: "It's a short form of the method we use when we assess a team."
2. **"A claim counts".** You call them unreliable before they start. Replace that sentence with: "We count a rung only as far as the foundations hold it up."
3. **The closing block.** Same markdown problem as the result. Replace the middle two sentences with: "If your people are new to AI, we'll start with the basics: briefing it well, writing down the context it needs, and organising the work so it builds up. If you're ready for more, we'll pick a workflow and build a working prototype of it."
4. **"Against your engagement".** A cold visitor has not agreed to an engagement. Keep the terms, change the words: "Credited in full if you go ahead with a later piece of work with us."

## The three changes that matter

1. **Question 8.** It is the drop-off. A shared login reads as progress, and the last two options are not choosable by a CEO. Junk answers here move the ceiling that caps every area.
2. **Rungs 1 and 2 on the four area questions.** Every buyer hits this four times. The overlap decides whether the meaning line says "a search engine, now and then" or "people use it to help". The first of those feels unread, and unread results do not book.
3. **Question 5's denominator.** As written, it scores a non-software firm on its whole headcount. That is the buyer you said you want, and it pushes them into a basics day their office may not need.
