# AI check-up: copy review (clarity, scannability, conversion)

Taken blind as a typical buyer: the COO of a 120-person services firm. Most staff have ChatGPT or Copilot, a few use it daily, the CTO "looks after AI", and nothing is measured. No em dashes found. British spelling holds throughout.

## The 12 questions

**Q1 to Q4 (area questions).** I picked 2, 2, 1, 1. I hesitated between 1 and 2 on every one. Rung 1's detail ("people chat with AI on their own") and rung 2's ("AI drafts, summarises and researches for the people doing the work") describe the same act: someone drafting an email in ChatGPT. What actually separates them is whether it happens routinely, and neither line says so. The labels are nicely parallel ("It helps people / It does steps / It runs..."). "Agents" in rung 4 and 5 is undefined, so a non-technical buyer can't tell "does steps" from "runs the workflow". In rung 3, the "it" in "people start and finish it" is unclear. The colon in the question text ("your core work: what you make or deliver") reads like a typo on a phone.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- Question: "How far does AI go in your core work (what you make or deliver)?" Use the same pattern for the other three.
- [1] It isn't part of the work / *Not used, or a few people try a chat tool now and then*
- [2] It helps people / *People routinely use AI to draft, summarise and research*
- [3] It does steps / *AI does some steps of the workflow; people start and finish the work*
- [4] It runs the workflow / *AI agents (software that acts on its own) start and run it; people decide at set points*
- [5] KEEP.
- Only Q1 needs the definition in brackets.

**Q5 (weekly use).** I picked 2, with no hesitation, though it's a guess. "Beyond the odd chat" is a good guardrail. The bands meet at 20, 40, 60 and 80, which is minor. **KEEP.**

**Q6 (ownership).** I hesitated between "Informal champions" and "Someone, part-time". Our CTO owns AI on top of his day job, so which one is he? "Champions" is consultancy-speak. The difference that matters is whether the person is named.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- [1] Nobody yet
- [2] A few enthusiasts, unofficially
- [4] A named person, alongside their main job
- [5] A named leader or team, with a budget

**Q7 (business context).** I picked 1, but I nearly picked 3 because we've uploaded files to a ChatGPT Project. "Connected" suggests IT work, so people who uploaded files can't tell where they fit. Option 5 is long and is clearly the "right" answer, which invites flattery.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- [1] Whatever someone pastes in
- [3] Some documents we've uploaded or connected
- [5] Our documents, systems and know-how, kept up to date

**Q8 (systems access).** This is the weakest question. "Broad keys", "scoped access" and "audit trail" are jargon to this buyer. It's also a flattery trap. The most common real situation is AI built into tools we already pay for (Copilot in Microsoft 365, AI in the CRM). Those buyers will pick "approved integrations" and score 4 by default. I picked 4 for that reason, and I shouldn't have.
Verdict: **REWRITE**
- Question: "How does AI get at your systems and data?"
- [1] It doesn't: people copy and paste
- [1] Through shared logins or keys that open everything
- [3] Through AI built into tools we already use
- [4] Through connections we've set up and approved
- [5] Through connections that reach only what they need, with a record of what AI did
- Scoring note: the new [3] is a value the founder needs to approve.

**Q9 (checking).** I picked 1. "Before it counts" is vague, and "before it's used" is clearer. Option 1 should admit that sometimes nobody checks.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- Question: "How is AI's work checked before it's used?"
- [1] Each person checks their own, or doesn't
- [3] A person reviews everything
- [5] Automatic checks, with people signing off at set points

**Q10 (measurement).** I picked 1 with no hesitation. The question is yes/no but the options aren't. "Baseline" is jargon.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- Question: "How do you measure what AI changes?"
- [1] We don't
- [3] Rough estimates
- [5] Per workflow, compared with how it ran before

**Q11 (rules).** I hesitated. We have an IT email saying "don't paste client data into ChatGPT". That isn't "none", and it isn't "a written policy" either.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- [1] None, or informal guidance
- [3] A written policy
- [5] KEEP.

**Q12 (workflow changed).** I picked 2. "Production" is engineering jargon. Option 2 also misses a pilot that is running right now.
Verdict: **TWEAK**
- Question: "Has AI changed how any recurring work runs, not just how fast people do it?"
- [1] Not yet
- [2] We've tried pilots, but none is in daily use
- [4] Yes, one workflow
- [5] Yes, several workflows run differently now

## Result copy

**AI-absent, rung 1.** "At most, some use a chat tool now and then, much like a search engine." This reads as a put-down, and it can contradict a Q5 answer of 20 to 40%. Rewrite: "AI isn't part of how the work gets done yet. Where people use it, it's mostly for one-off questions."

**AI-absent, rung 2.** Lands well. KEEP.

**Close to AI-enabled.** This line is wrong for the person reading it. They have just said AI *does steps* in at least one area. "You have AI tools, or a mandate to get them" undersells them and reads like a different result. Rewrite: "AI already does steps of the work in at least one area. What's missing is a workflow that agents start and run."

**AI-enabled / AI-native.** Both are fine. KEEP.

**Gaps line.** It states the problem but not the consequence, so it reads like an error. Make sure the singular and plural forms really render; "claim(s)" must never appear on screen. Rewrite: "2 of your 4 areas claim more than your foundations support, so we've counted them at the lower rung." For one area, use "1 of your 4 areas claims more...".

**Next step, AI-absent.** "Writing the context AI needs in markdown" will lose a non-technical CEO. Rewrite: "Start with the basics. In one focused day we'll get your people prompting well, writing down the business context AI needs, and organising their work in projects so it builds up over time."

**Next step, close.** "Pick it with you" is clunky. Rewrite: "You're one workflow away from AI-enabled. In one focused day we'll choose it with you and build a working prototype."

**CTA note.** "Credited in full against your engagement." This assumes an engagement the buyer hasn't agreed to, and it never says the session is paid. A buyer who finds the price on /book will feel misled. Rewrite: "A paid day, credited in full if you go on to work with us." The button itself is the right next step. KEEP "Book a working session →".

## Page copy: top issues

1. **Privacy is buried in the method paragraph.** It's the strongest reason to start, so move it into the intro. Add: "No sign-up. Your answers stay in your browser."
2. **The method paragraph is dense and partly wrong.** "Agents running a workflow counts if..." is ungrammatical, and it leaves out the 3.5 threshold for "does steps". "Generalised from the agentic maturity model in our team assessment" is inside-baseball. Rewrite: "A rung counts only if your foundations hold it up. 'Does steps' needs a foundations average of 3.5 out of 5. 'Runs the workflow' needs 4. 'Improves the workflow' needs 4.5 and real measurement. Where an area claims more, we count the rung your foundations support. The method comes from the maturity model we use when we assess teams."
3. **"Markdown" in "Start from where you are".** Rewrite: "...prompting well, writing down the context AI needs and organising work in projects."
4. **The credit line under the page CTA** has the same problem as on the result screen. Use the same rewrite.
5. **The AI-native definition** ends with "and you score 4 out of 5 or more". That's scoring mechanics inside a definition. Move it to the method paragraph and stop the line at "your foundations back that up."

## The 3 changes that matter most

1. **Fix the "close to AI-enabled" meaning line.** It currently tells the warmest leads something that doesn't match their answers, at the moment they decide whether to book.
2. **Rewrite Q8 and define "agents" once in Q1.** Q8 is the clearest case where jargon and a missing option push typical buyers to over-claim. Undefined "agents" muddies all four area questions.
3. **Make the CTA honest and plain.** Say the day is paid and the credit is conditional, and remove "markdown" from both next-step lines. Being upfront about price now avoids a drop-off on /book later.
