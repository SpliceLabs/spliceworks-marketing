# AI check-up: plain-language review

Reviewer lens: a busy CEO or COO, not technical, reading on a phone. Will they get each line on the first read, and would two leaders at the same company read it the same way?

## Headline findings

1. **One idea, many names.** The tool switches between "AI", "AI tools", "AI agents" and "agents". It also switches between "the work", "workflow", "recurring work" and "core work", and between "people", "your people", "desk-based people" and "keen people". Readers will assume each new word means something new.
2. **Four words do most of the damage:** *workflow*, *agent*, *foundations* and *rung*. None is defined where the reader first meets it. *Set points* sounds like a thermostat setting.
3. **Rung 4's detail line is the hardest line in the tool,** and it repeats in four of the twelve questions.
4. **The gaps line on the result screen** ("ahead of what your foundations can support, so we've counted the lower rung") is the line people will most often read twice. It is also the line that explains their score.
5. **Fix:** say "AI" throughout the questions and "workflow" for a piece of work. Define both once. Say "agent" only where it is defined. Replace "rung" with "level".

## Term consistency: pick one, use it everywhere

| Concept | Variants now | Use |
|---|---|---|
| The technology | AI, AI tools, AI agents, agents, chat tool | **AI** in questions; **AI agent** only with its definition |
| A repeated job | the work, workflow, recurring work, core work | **workflow** (defined) |
| Staff | people, your people, desk-based people, keen people | **your people**; "people who work mostly at a computer" for Q5 and the page |
| Human control | decide at set points, points that matter, signing off at set points | **people make the key decisions** |

## The questions

### Q1 to Q4: area stems

Stem: "How far does AI go in {area} on a normal week?"

- "on a normal week" is American usage. British usage is "in a normal week".
- Q1's parenthesis "(what you make or deliver)" breaks the flow.
- Readers will split on whether to answer for their best example or the typical case. Add one hint line under the stem and use it in all four questions.

Rewrites:
- Q1: "In a normal week, how far does AI go in your core work, the things you make or deliver?"
- Q2 to Q4: "In a normal week, how far does AI go in {sales, marketing and support}?"
- Hint (all four): "Pick the furthest it goes in regular work, not one-off trials."

### The five levels (shared by Q1 to Q4)

The labels are mostly parallel: each starts with "It" plus a verb. The detail lines aren't parallel, and two use semicolons.

| Level | Now | Issue | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 label | It isn't part of the work | Clear but negative | **KEEP** |
| 1 detail | Not used, or a few people try a chat tool now and then | "Chat tool" is vague | "Not used, or a few people try ChatGPT or similar now and then" |
| 2 label | It helps people | Clear | **KEEP** |
| 2 detail | People routinely use AI to draft, summarise and research | "Routinely" can mean daily or monthly, and Q5 says "every week" | "Most weeks, people use AI to draft, summarise or look things up" |
| 3 label | It does steps | Reads as clipped | "It does some steps" |
| 3 detail | AI does set steps of the work; people start and finish it | "Set steps" is ambiguous (fixed? set up?), plus a semicolon and an unclear "it" | "AI does some steps by itself. People still start and finish the work." |
| 4 label | It runs the workflow | "Workflow" is undefined | "It runs the workflow" (KEEP once workflow is defined) |
| 4 detail | AI agents, software that acts on its own, start and run it; people decide at set points | Clause inside a clause, semicolon, two jargon terms, unclear "it". Needs a second read | "AI starts the work and sees it through. People make the key decisions." |
| 5 label | It improves the workflow | Fine | "It runs and improves the workflow" (makes clear it includes level 4) |
| 5 detail | Agents run it end to end and improve it from results; people set the goals | Switches to "agents"; "from results" is vague | "AI runs it from start to finish and improves it based on results. People set the goals." |

Put the definition of "agent" in the glossary, not in the level 4 detail line.

### Before Q5: add a bridging line

The tool moves from areas to foundations with no signal. Add a line above Q5: "Next, 8 questions on your foundations: the basics that let AI take on more."

### Q5 adoption

"Of the people whose work is mostly documents, email or a computer, how many use AI in that work every week, beyond the odd chat?"

- The sentence is 26 words long, the subject comes late and the clauses are stacked.
- "Beyond the odd chat" is unclear.
- "How many" asks for a count, but the options are percentages.
- The bands overlap: a reader at exactly 40% has two answers.

Rewrite: "Think of your people who work mostly at a computer. Roughly what share use AI in their work every week, not just now and then?"

Options: "Under 20%", "20 to 39%", "40 to 59%", "60 to 79%", "80% or more".

### Q6 ownership

"Who owns AI at your company?" Readers will take "owns" to mean different things: the licences, the budget or the strategy.

Rewrite: "Who is in charge of AI at your company?"

- "Nobody yet": KEEP. Readers may confuse it with option 2, so the next rewrite separates them.
- "Keen people, but it's nobody's job" → "Some keen people, but nobody is in charge"
- "A named person, alongside their main job" → "One person, on top of their main job"
- "A named leader or team, with time and a budget" → "A leader or team, with time and budget set aside"

### Q7 context

"When AI helps with your work, what does it know about your business?" **KEEP.**

- "Only what someone pastes in each time": **KEEP**
- "Some notes or documents we've uploaded or connected": "connected" is jargon, and the double "or" slows the reader. → "Some documents we've given it"
- "Our documents, data and experts' know-how, kept up to date": readers will ask whose experts. → "Our documents, data and what our best people know, kept up to date"

### Q8 access

"How does AI get at your systems and data?" "Get at" is loose, and "systems" is vague. → "How does AI reach your company's software and data?"

Add a hint: "If more than one fits, pick the one that covers most of your AI use." Without it, two readers at the same firm will pick different answers.

- "It doesn't: people copy and paste": **KEEP**
- "Through AI built into tools we already use" → "Through AI built into tools we already use, like Copilot in Microsoft 365"
- "Through connections we've set up and approved": "connections" is jargon. → "Through links to our systems that we've set up and approved"
- "Through connections that reach only what each task needs, with a record of what AI did": 15 words, too long for a phone. → "Through approved links that open only what each task needs, with a log of what AI did"
- "Through a shared login, or one that can see far too much": "one" is unclear. → "Through a shared password, or an account that can see far more than it needs"
- Detail "Riskier than copy and paste, not further along": "further along" is our internal framing. → "This is riskier than copy and paste."

### Q9 checking

"How is AI's work checked before it's used?" The sentence is passive. → "How do you check AI's work before anyone relies on it?"

The options mix clauses and a noun phrase. Make all three full sentences:

- "Each person checks their own, or doesn't" (needs a second read) → "Each person checks their own, if they check at all"
- "Someone reviews it before it's used" → "Someone else checks it first"
- "Automatic checks, with people signing off at set points" → "Software checks it automatically, and a person signs off key decisions"

### Q10 measurement

"How do you measure what AI changes?" This can mean "which things it changes" or "how much difference it makes". → "How do you measure the difference AI makes?"

Make the options parallel:

- "We don't": **KEEP**
- "Rough estimates" → "We estimate it roughly"
- "Per workflow, compared with how it ran before" → "We measure each workflow against how it ran before"

### Q11 rules

"What rules cover AI use?" → "What rules do you have for using AI?"

- "None, or informal guidance" → "None, or only informal advice"
- "A written policy": **KEEP**
- "Approved tools, data rules, spend limits and a way to switch an agent off": readers won't know whether they need all four items, and "agent" is undefined. → "A written policy, plus approved tools, data rules, spending limits and a way to switch AI off"

### Q12 workflow change

"Has AI changed how any recurring work runs, not just how fast people do it?" "Recurring work" is a new term, and the contrast needs a second read. → "Has AI changed the steps of any regular workflow, not just made people faster?"

- "Not yet": **KEEP**
- "We've tried pilots, but none is in daily use" → "We've run trials, but none is in daily use"
- "Yes, one workflow" → "Yes, one workflow now runs differently"
- "Yes, several workflows run differently now" → "Yes, several workflows now run differently"

## Page copy: top issues

1. **Intro:** "whether your foundations back that up". This is the first use of "foundations" and it isn't defined. → "and whether the basics are in place to support it."
2. **"Each area sits on one of five rungs"** → "We place each area at one of five levels:"
3. **The foundations paragraph** is one sentence of about 60 words listing eight items. Make it a bulleted list after "Eight more questions check your foundations:".
4. **Threshold paragraph:** "We count a rung only as far as your foundations hold it up" needs a second read. "Real measurement" is vague. Rewrite: "Each level needs strong enough foundations. 'Does some steps' needs an average of 3.5 out of 5. 'Runs the workflow' needs 4. 'Runs and improves' needs 4.5, plus the top answer on measurement. If an area claims more, we score it at the level its foundations support."
5. **Worked example:** "only informal champions own it" is jargon. "Those foundations back AI helping people, not doing steps, so two areas go further than their foundations support" needs a second read. → "a few keen people lead it, but nobody is in charge and nothing is measured. That's enough for 'helps people' but not 'does some steps', so two areas score lower than claimed."
6. **FAQ:** "critical workflow" appears only here. Elsewhere it is "at least one workflow". Use one phrase.
7. **"Desk-based people"** → "people who work mostly at a computer", to match Q5.
8. **"Start from where you are":** this section repeats "briefing AI well... builds up". Use the same rewrite as the result screen.

## Reading level

These are rough estimates by hand, not computed (unverified).

- **Questions:** most stems are 6 to 12 words, around reading age 11 to 13 (US grade 6 to 8). These lines push it up to about reading age 17:
  - Q5 (26 words)
  - level 4 detail
  - Q8 option 4
  - Q11 option 3
  - Q12
  With the rewrites above, the questions should sit at about reading age 12.
- **Page:** about reading age 15 to 17 (grade 10 to 12). The foundations paragraph, the threshold paragraph and the worked example push it up, along with the jargon density (foundations, rung, workflow). Splitting those three paragraphs and replacing "rung" should bring it to about reading age 13 to 14.
- **Result screen:** about reading age 14, driven by the gaps line and the basics next step.

## Glossary: the terms the check-up can't avoid

- **Workflow:** a piece of work your company does again and again, from start to finish, like handling a new order or closing the month's accounts.
  Where: hint under Q1, glossary on the intro, and the first use on the page.
- **AI agent:** AI that carries out tasks by itself, not just answering questions.
  Where: the result screen (AI-enabled and AI-native meanings), the "Where you can land" section and the glossary. Keep it out of the questions and say "AI" there.
- **Foundations:** the basics that let AI take on more: someone in charge, checks, rules and measurement.
  Where: the bridging line before Q5, the page intro and the gaps line.
- **Level:** how far AI goes in an area, from "isn't part of the work" (1) to "runs and improves the workflow" (5). This replaces "rung".
  Where: the "How the check-up works" section and the gaps line.

Show the glossary as a short "Words we use" block above Question 1, and repeat each definition at the point of first use listed above. On a phone, a definition two screens back is a definition never seen.
